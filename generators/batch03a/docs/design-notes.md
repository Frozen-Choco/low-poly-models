# Design and construction

These are original category interpretations using plausible artist-chosen domestic dimensions, not surveyed or licensed manufacturer copies. No reference photography is redistributed and no claim of manufacturing precision is made.

- Kettle: approximately 20 cm body diameter, 24 cm raised handle height. Continuous stainless outer/inner shell with 2 mm nominal sidewall; a boolean through-opening connects the body to a tapered 1.4 mm-wall spout. Lid rests on the neck, includes a genuine small steam vent and insulated knob. Insulating carry arch is connected to two metal ears/pivots. Open spout end is real geometry. The arch uses a stable frame and 24-sided cross-section.
- Rice bowl: 12.5 cm rim diameter, 6.75 cm height. Continuous lathed outer and inner wall with rounded lip and integrated raised foot. Unglazed contact ring and three indigo underglaze fine lines distinguish the rim. Empty interior is modeled all the way to the well, no flat disk across the opening. Subtle warm ivory glaze and matte foot are standard glTF PBR materials.
- Cutting board: 34 x 20.5 x 2.4 cm. Single solid board with 5 mm softened edges. Original packed long-grain texture aligns lengthwise, while end faces use a second original growth-ring texture. No fake handle, surface sticker, logo, or inaccessible photo dependencies. Grain is colored wood texture rather than excessive mesh displacement.

Portable material boundary: photographic reflection detail comes from the lighting and smooth geometry; no unsupported procedural shader nodes are required in GLB. The board textures are generated numerically then packed and exported. The bowl is deliberately glazed, and the handle is deliberately heat-resistant dark polymer; these materials should not be misread as an all-plastic kettle.

Context: objects sit on a modeled stone kitchen countertop; the bowl sits on the 24 mm board top. Alternate illumination changes light direction and color. Background wall/tile joints and counter are context-only and excluded from individual asset GLBs and source Blends.
